\section*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
Medical vision-language models can produce fluent, correct-looking answers without using the clinical visual evidence those answers require. This dissertation develops two complementary threads, \emph{Trustworthy} and \emph{Efficient}, to make evidence use observable and practical under clinical resource constraints. The research arc aligns representations to clinical evidence, reasons efficiently through selective perception, audits behavior when evidence fails, interrogates whether encoded concepts control answers, and re-grounds answering in localized evidence. HybridMED, EGMA, and EG-SpikeFormer use report structure or clinician gaze for alignment, with parameter-sharing, auxiliary-data, and energy considerations. ViThinker studies economical visual querying. MedVIGIL audits silent failures under broken evidence and scales evaluation through computed labels; RAW distinguishes readable concepts from concepts that control answers. Proposed Evidence-Derived Answering and Medical Findings Are Prediction Errors connect localized evidence to spatial answers and finding recognition while reducing perception or lesion-label demands. Together these studies treat accuracy, evidence use, energy, parameters, perceptual budget, labels, and annotation cost as related but distinct dimensions of medical AI design.
