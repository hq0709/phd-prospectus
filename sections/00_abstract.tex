\section*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
Medical vision-language models can produce fluent, correct-looking answers without using the clinical visual evidence those answers require. This dissertation develops two complementary threads, \emph{Trustworthy} and \emph{Efficient}, to make evidence use observable and practical under clinical resource constraints. First, it aligns image representations with clinical evidence, using the structure of radiology reports and the gaze of clinicians as supervision, while reducing parameter, data, and energy costs. Second, it shows that a model can reason accurately while looking at only a few selected image regions. Third, it builds an audit that detects silent failures, cases where a model keeps answering confidently after its visual evidence has been removed or corrupted, and extends that audit to 3D scans with automatically computed labels. Fourth, it tests whether a clinical concept that can be read out of a model's internal representation also controls the model's answer, and finds that on chest radiographs it often does not. The proposed work closes the loop with two methods that tie each answer to localized image evidence: one derives spatial answers directly from the point the model selects, and the other finds abnormalities as regions a model of normal anatomy fails to predict, which reduces the need for lesion annotations.
