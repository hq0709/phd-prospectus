\section*{Abstract}\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
Medical vision-language models can produce fluent, correct-looking answers without using the clinical visual evidence those answers require. This dissertation develops two complementary threads, \emph{Trustworthy} and \emph{Efficient}, to make evidence use observable and practical under clinical resource constraints. The work aligns representations to clinical evidence and studies selective perception. It then audits failures, tests whether encoded concepts control answers, and connects answers to localized evidence. HybridMED, EGMA, and EG-SpikeFormer use report structure or clinician gaze for alignment. The studies also examine parameter sharing, auxiliary data, and energy, respectively. ViThinker studies economical visual querying. MedVIGIL audits silent failures under broken evidence and scales evaluation through computed labels; \emph{Is Decodability Enough? Rethinking Concept-Level Control in Medical Vision-Language Models} (the project codename RAW) distinguishes readable concepts from concepts that control answers. Proposed Evidence-Derived Answering and Medical Findings Are Prediction Errors connect localized evidence to spatial answers and finding recognition while reducing perception or lesion-label demands.
